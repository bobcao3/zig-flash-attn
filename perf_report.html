MHA and GQA batch sequences of the given length (GQA rounds the query heads to a multiple of 4 and shares each key-value head among 4 of them). Varlen draws sequence lengths uniformly between ½× and 1½× the given mean. Sliding window runs one 16384-token sequence with the given window: causal sees that many keys to the left, dense also that many to the right. The reference is cuDNN where cuDNN runs the shape (batched through PyTorch SDPA, varlen through the cuDNN attention op with ragged offsets) and PyTorch otherwise: SDPA for batched shapes on GB10, torch.nn.attention.varlen.varlen_attn for varlen and sliding window (cuDNN has no window here, and on GB10 it has no varlen backward). FA4 (CuTe) runs its own varlen and window kernels on the same cases; its SM100 backward rejects local windows at head dim 256. Each percentage divides Zig by the faster of the reference and FA4 at that point, or by the reference alone where FA4 has no measurement. Inter-sequence attention kernels

Inter-sequence attention kernels pack many short sequences of different lengths end to end on one token axis, and a visibility rule lets each query sequence attend across sequence boundaries to the keys of every sequence it may see. On short-sequence varlen batches they beat cuDNN and FA4 by a wide margin; each figure below shows one technique behind that.
